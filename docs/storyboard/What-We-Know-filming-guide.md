# What We Know — 60-second filming guide

## Concept

A question reaches a colleague the author would not have known to ask. A useful online exchange becomes a natural connection in the office.

Use one shared office and three performers. Cast colleagues of different ages without assigning skills by generation. Maya is a newcomer who already has design experience. Christine contributes patient-service knowledge. Jonas explains the history of a technical decision and becomes interested in Maya's approach.

## Shot list

| Shot | Time | Camera and action | Sound / readable text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 s | Wide office. Maya, Christine and Jonas work at separate desks within the same space. | Office ambience. No exposition. |
| 2 | 4–8 s | Close detail: Maya pauses between her portal prototype and draft research question. | Pencil tap, keyboard. |
| 3 | 8–13 s | Over Maya's shoulder as she starts a What We Know post. | “Could someone outside our team take a look?” |
| 4 | 13–18 s | Screen recording: context, prototype attachment and research questions. | Show what colleagues can review. Avoid a wall of text. |
| 5 | 18–23 s | Medium shot of Christine recognizing the issue in the post. | A small reaction, then typing. |
| 6 | 23–28 s | Screen insert of Christine's contribution; notification on Maya's screen. | “Patients ask us which instructions apply.” |
| 7 | 28–33 s | Jonas opens an earlier project and begins his reply. | Office sound; a change of angle suggests time passing. |
| 8 | 33–38 s | Maya opens his linked experience. | “Why we built one shared page.” Show the explanation, not only the notification. |
| 9 | 38–44 s | Top-down or screen insert: Maya revises the study task. | “Start with the appointment confirmation.” |
| 10 | 44–50 s | Two-shot at a shared table, later in the workday. | Maya: “Jonas? Thanks for sharing that.” Jonas: “What are you trying next?” |
| 11 | 50–56 s | Maya shows her research approach. Jonas is curious; Christine briefly joins. Cut to Maya's author update. | “Here is what changed.” Natural conversation can sit beneath the shot. |
| 12 | 56–60 s | Product end card. | What We Know — Another perspective on the work in front of you. |

## Production notes

- The 60-second limit includes the end card. Aim for a 58–59 second export to leave a margin.
- Use screen recordings from the actual prototype for readable product details; storyboard interface sketches are composition guides.
- Avoid internal-monologue voiceovers and staged celebration. Brief dialogue and gestures should carry the social payoff.
- Notifications suggest discovery, not guaranteed immediate responses. Show a time transition.
- Make the change to the research task visible. The value is a contribution influencing work, not simply messages arriving.
- Keep both sides active: Jonas supplies context; Maya contributes a research approach he can learn about.
- Label the film “Illustrative scenario” unobtrusively near the opening. All company, people and healthcare content are fictional.
- One location, three performers and desk inserts are sufficient. Record dialogue separately if the office is noisy.

## Context for continuing in a ChatGPT chat

I am Jenn, a Gen Z designer studying a Master's in Interaction Design at SUPSI, with about three years in a creative and e-commerce team doing graphic and UX/UI design. I am entering the SUPSI LNOC 2026 competition about joining an intergenerational workplace. Deliverables: a written proposal of maximum 500 words and a video of maximum 60 seconds. Criteria emphasize intergenerational relevance, connection to my field, employee/company perspectives, concreteness and originality.

The concept is What We Know: an internal hub where employees post work in progress with context and linked materials, invite relevant perspectives outside their immediate team, discuss the work, then explain what changed. Its MVP is contextual request → discussion → author update. Participation supports everyday work without requiring a recurring mentoring relationship. It should show reciprocal contribution, rather than older colleagues only teaching younger ones.
